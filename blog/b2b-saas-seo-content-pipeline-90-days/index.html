Content & SEO

How we built a full SEO and content pipeline for a B2B SaaS product in 90 days

Compound. May 2026 4 min read Issue 01
+30%
Organic traffic growth
90
Days to a live channel
12
Keyword clusters identified

Most B2B SaaS brands treat SEO as a background task. Something the content person handles. Something that compounds over time without much input. That assumption is why most of them have organic traffic that flatlines for years.

When our founder started working with a B2B software product, the situation was familiar: a solid product, a small but capable team, and an organic channel that was technically live but functionally invisible. No rankings for anything that mattered commercially. Blog content that answered questions nobody was asking. A homepage that Google could read but had no reason to care about.

Ninety days later, organic was a functioning acquisition channel. Here is what the build actually looked like.

The audit came first. Everything else came from it.

The first two weeks were not about publishing anything. They were about understanding what was broken and why. Three things stood out immediately:

The content was disconnected from the buying journey. Posts existed, but they targeted awareness-stage curiosity rather than decision-stage intent. Someone researching a specific integration, comparing pricing models, or trying to solve a specific technical problem could not find this brand in search. The content was informative. It was not commercial.

The site architecture sent no clear signals to Google. There was no internal linking logic. Pages that should have carried the most authority, the core product and feature pages, were sitting in isolation. The blog was not pointing equity anywhere useful.

Keyword targeting was based on volume, not intent. High-volume terms with broad intent had been chased. Lower-volume terms with high commercial intent, the kind a buyer types when they are close to a decision, had been ignored entirely.

None of this is unusual. It is the default state of most SaaS content programmes that were started without a search-first brief.

What the 90-day build actually covered

01 — Weeks 1–2
Foundation and website
Rewrote title tags and meta descriptions. Built an internal linking map. Identified 12 keyword clusters. Updated the website for both SEO and CRO simultaneously.
02 — Weeks 3–6
Content and user guides
Built a content calendar per product category. Rewrote all user guides around how customers actually search. Produced two to three content pieces per week across each category series.
03 — Weeks 7–12
Email and pipeline
Re-engaged 7,000 dormant email subscribers. Built separate flows for free users, trial users, and paid customers. By week twelve, organic was generating trial signups and email was converting.

Product categorisation before anything else

Before writing a single piece of content, we categorised the product. The software served multiple distinct use cases and user types, but the existing content treated everything as one undifferentiated audience. We mapped each product category to its own keyword cluster, its own content series, and its own email flow. A free user researching one feature needed a completely different journey to a paying customer on a different module. Getting this right first meant every piece of content and every email had a clear audience and a clear commercial job.

User guides rebuilt around search intent

The existing user guides were written from the inside out, structured around how the product was built rather than how customers described their problems. We rewrote them around the actual search queries that support tickets and search console data were surfacing. Guides that previously sat on page four ranked in the top three for their target queries within six weeks.

7,000 email subscribers re-engaged with segmented flows

The product had a list of 7,000 subscribers that had received almost no communication. Rather than a single blast, we segmented by product usage and built three separate flows: one for active free users nudging toward a trial, one for lapsed users with a specific reactivation hook, and one for paid customers focused on feature adoption and expansion. Open rates on the re-engagement sequence hit 45%.

01
Bottom-of-funnel comparison content. The searches a buyer does when they already know the category and are evaluating options. Most SaaS brands avoid these because they feel aggressive. That is exactly why they work.
02
Integration and use-case pages. Specific pages targeting the exact workflows a prospect might search, written with the depth of a landing page rather than the looseness of a blog post.
03
Problem-aware content per category. Posts written in the language a frustrated user actually types, not the language a product team uses internally, and mapped to the specific product category most relevant to that pain point.

What actually drove the result?

Not the volume of content. The intent match.

Every piece had a specific commercial job. The brief defined that job before the writer started. That is the difference between a content programme that builds an organic channel and one that builds an archive of posts nobody reads.

The sequence that matters

Infrastructure before content. Internal linking before new pages. High-intent terms before high-volume terms. Product categories mapped before a single brief is written. Most content programmes do this in reverse and wonder why nothing moves.

What to take from this

If your SaaS product has an organic channel that is not converting, the problem is almost never the quality of the writing. It is one of three things: the wrong keyword targets, missing infrastructure, or content that serves curiosity rather than intent.

Fix those three things in that order. Map your product to audience segments before you start. Then build the email flows that make the content work harder. Then publish.
